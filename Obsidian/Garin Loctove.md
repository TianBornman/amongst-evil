# Garin Loctove

*The Grand Forger. The God-Smith. He Who Measured the End.*

> *"His eyes saw the pattern of eternity, and in his final act, he built the Clock to measure the world's allotted time."*

Garin Loctove is the being who built the **[[Grand Clock]]** — the colossal hourglass that towers over the shattered plains of [[Kareth Vall]] and measures the world's remaining time.

He is called the **Grand Forger** in Brotherhood records. Some texts call him a **god-smith** — not a god, but something that worked at the level of gods: a craftsman whose materials were the threads of existence itself.

---

## The Forging

Loctove did not build the Grand Clock with tools. He built it with **the essence of magick itself** — pulling threads of life from the fabric of the world and tempering them into metal.

The result:
- A structure of **bronze and obsidian gears** fused with twin glass chambers spanning mountains
- Filled with **shimmering golden sand** — not natural sand; widely theorised by Brotherhood scholars to be condensed time itself
- A heartbeat — a **low, resonant chime** audible across leagues, sounding every hour

The Clock was not manufactured. It was *grown* from the world's own substance.

---

## Intent — Two Traditions

Two traditions survive about why Loctove built the Clock, and the Brotherhood has never resolved the contradiction:

**The Gift Theory:**
Loctove built the Grand Clock as a gift to the gods — a monument to the divine order of time, a celebration of eternity's structure made physical.

**The Defiance Theory:**
Loctove built it to defy the gods — a reminder that even divinity ends when the last grain falls. A declaration that mortality and limitation apply to all things, divine or otherwise.

> [!info] Open Question
> Neither tradition agrees on whether the Clock's eventual silence is **salvation** or **annihilation**. This ambiguity is intentional — the Brotherhood records both and commits to neither. Loctove's own writings, if they existed, have not survived.

---

## His Fate

The Brotherhood's records do not clearly state what became of Loctove. The phrase *"in his final act, he built the Clock"* suggests the Grand Clock was the last thing he made — possibly the last thing he did.

Whether he died in the making, transcended it, or simply stopped — unknown.

> [!note]
> Some sealed tomes in the [[Crypt of Knowledge]] are attributed to an author called *"The Forger's Remnant"* — a designation that appears nowhere else in Brotherhood records. Whether this refers to a follower, a copy, or something of Loctove himself that persisted after his final act has never been verified.

---

## Significance to the Brotherhood

Loctove is not worshipped by the Brotherhood. He is **studied**.

The Grand Clock he built is the measure of everything the Brotherhood fights for — every mission run, every Brother fallen, every Ichor Core processed at camp is in service of slowing what he set in motion. Whether he built the Clock as a gift or a warning, the Brotherhood uses it as a **compass**: a fixed point against which they measure whether they are succeeding or failing in their purpose.

The Third Oath — *"We are the hand that slows the fall of the hourglass"* — is, implicitly, a response to Loctove's creation.
