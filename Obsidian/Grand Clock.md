# The Grand Clock

> [!quote] Third Oath of the Brotherhood
> "We are the hand that slows the fall of the hourglass."

The Grand Clock is not a metaphor.

It towers upon the shattered plains of **[[Kareth Vall]]** — a relic from before memory, a structure so vast it scrapes the heavens. From afar it appears as a colossus of bronze and obsidian gears, fused with twin glass chambers that span mountains, filled with shimmering golden sand. The upper chamber bleeds endlessly into the lower. None can say from where the sands come — or where they go.

Its heartbeat is audible across leagues: a low, resonant chime that echoes through the earth every hour.

The learned claim each chime marks the shortening of existence itself.

---

## Origin — The Forging

The Grand Clock was crafted by **[[Garin Loctove]]**, the Grand Forger — said to have built not with tools but with *the essence of magick itself*, pulling threads of life from the fabric of the world and tempering them into metal.

His eyes saw the pattern of eternity. In his final act, he built the Clock to *measure the world's allotted time.*

Two traditions survive about his intent:
- Some say Loctove built it as a **gift to the gods** — a monument to the divine order of time
- Others believe he built it to **defy them** — a reminder that even divinity ends when the last grain falls

Neither tradition agrees on whether the Clock's eventual silence is salvation or annihilation.

---

## The Acceleration

When [[Akalaer]] died — struck down by [[Alexandr the Iron Tyrant|Alexandr the Iron Tyrant]] — the Grand Clock trembled.

Witnesses described **storms of golden dust** rising from [[Kareth Vall]], and the chimes growing uneven, as if the Clock itself wept. Since then, its tempo has quickened. Every scholar, priest, and prophet who has studied it agrees: *the sands are running out.*

What the Clock measured before Akalaer's death is unknown. What it measures now is the time remaining to a world running on stolen time — accelerated by:
- Each act of corruption
- Each death at the hands of the Curse
- Each sin of the crown that started it all

The more the Curse spreads, the faster the Clock runs. The two are not metaphorically connected — they are mechanically bound. The Brotherhood suspects Loctove knew this. They do not know if he intended it.

---

## The Brotherhood's Vigil

The Brotherhood watches the Clock from afar. They record its every movement, its every sound. They know that when the last grain falls, time itself will collapse — and the world will follow.

> *"The kings and scholars saw doom. The Brotherhood saw purpose."*

This is the core of their existence: not to win, not to defeat the Curse entirely — but to **delay**. To slow the fall of the sands long enough to learn how to stop what is coming, or to at least die knowing what it was.

The Brotherhood records the Clock's tempo in the **[[Crypt of Knowledge]]** — a running chronicle of how much time remains, and whether each generation has slowed or hastened the end.

> [!quote] Fourth Oath of the Brotherhood
> "In blood, we learn. In death, we remember. In darkness, we preserve."

## Grand Clock Pressure — In the Field

**Grand Clock Pressure** is the in-game mechanic that represents the Clock's acceleration felt at the local level. In cursed territory, the Clock's influence bleeds into reality — manifesting as accelerated enemy spawn rates, compressed wave timings, and the emergence of the **[[Combat#4.3 Hourglass Ichor (Special Spawn)|Hourglass Ichor]]**.

| Pressure Level | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Low** | Standard wave timing — Opening phase lasts full 2 minutes |
| **Moderate** | Wave phases compress — Surge reached 30–60 seconds earlier |
| **High** | Mission may reach Surge in under 3 minutes; Ichor spawns more frequently |
| **Critical** | Grand Toll phase triggered early; enemy density at maximum |

Missions with high Grand Clock Pressure are not harder by design — they are harder because the Clock is running faster there. These locations are closer to where the Curse is most active, most violent, and most connected to the temporal bleed.

> [!note]
> **Ichor Cores** recovered from Hourglass Ichor enemies can be processed at camp to marginally reduce Grand Clock Pressure in subsequent missions. This is one of the few ways the Brotherhood can push back against the Clock's acceleration — and why Ichor Cores are treated as priority material.

## The Council of Shadows Beneath the Clock

The **Council of Shadows** — the Brotherhood's governing body — convenes at [[Kareth Vall]] beneath the Grand Clock once per generation. The Clock's shadow falls over the proceedings. Its chime marks the opening and closing of each session.

This is not ceremony. The Brotherhood meets beneath the Clock to remind themselves what they are working against.

---

## The Grand Toll

The **Grand Toll** is the name the Brotherhood gives to the Clock's final chime — the moment the last grain of sand falls, and existence itself begins to unravel.

It is also the name given to the most intense phase of a mission's wave structure ([[Combat#3.3 Wave Structure|Combat §3.3]]) — the point where enemy spawns reach maximum intensity and no breaks remain. The naming is deliberate: when a mission reaches Grand Toll phase, the Brothers on the ground are experiencing, in miniature, what the world will feel when the Clock finally stops.

One of the Chaos mission types is named **"The Grand Toll"** — a mission set at the edge of a site so corrupted that the local time-bleed mirrors the Clock's terminal state.

## What the Clock Looks Like

For future art reference:
- **Structure:** Colossus of bronze and obsidian gears, fused with twin glass chambers
- **Scale:** The glass chambers span mountains — visible from leagues away
- **The Sand:** Shimmering golden — not natural sand; possibly condensed time itself
- **Sound:** A low, resonant chime every hour, audible across leagues; when trembling, chimes grow uneven
- **Location:** Shattered plains of [[Kareth Vall]]
- **State since Akalaer's death:** Running faster than intended; golden dust storms reported at the site
